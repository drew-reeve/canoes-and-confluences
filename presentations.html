In the field

This is where Laurie is happiest: teaching on the trail and on the river, where the historic sites are part of the adventure. She teaches on trips with outfitters including Lewis and Clark Trail Adventures, which has run trips along the expedition's route since 1989.

Crossing the Bitterroots: The Lolo Trail

Walk the Nee-Me-Poo Trail, the ancient Nimiipuu (Nez Perce) route through the Bitterroot Mountains that the Corps of Discovery followed in 1805 and called the hardest stretch of their journey. Laurie teaches the trail's long Nimiipuu history alongside the expedition's struggle and rescue, right where it happened.

On the river

Laurie is a featured historian aboard American Cruise Lines voyages on the Columbia and Snake Rivers, now heading into her third season.

Onboard lecture series: the Columbia and Snake

A series of talks that follows the ship upriver: the tribal nations of the Columbia Plateau, the age of exploration, the Lewis and Clark Expedition's passage, the fur trade, the Oregon Trail's final leg, and the dams and towns that remade the rivers. Each talk sets up what guests will see on shore.

The Age of Exploration in the Northwest

Spanish, British, Russian, and American voyages to the Northwest coast, the search for the Columbia, overland explorers, and the fur trade that followed, and what these encounters meant for the people already living here.

Homelands: The Tribal Nations of the Northwest

Long before any expedition, these rivers and mountains were home to the Nimiipuu (Nez Perce), Shoshone-Bannock, Coeur d'Alene, Salish, and many other peoples. Laurie centers their history, their relationships to the land, and their side of the stories usually told from the explorers' point of view.
